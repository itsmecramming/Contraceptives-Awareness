A. Overview & Physiology

Contraception is the tactical deployment of methods or devices to prevent pregnancy. Understanding the biological terrain is step one in effective risk management.

The Menstrual Cycle

On average, a cycle lasts 28 days. Ovulation typically occurs around day 14. This is the window of highest risk for unintended pregnancy.

B. Barrier Methods

Physical defenses designed to prevent sperm from reaching the egg. They represent the primary frontline defense against Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs).

Male Condoms +

Effectiveness: 85% (Typical Use) - 98% (Perfect Use)

Pros: Protects against STIs, easily accessible, no hormones.

Cons: Requires discipline before every deployment, risk of breakage.

Perfect Use
Female Condoms +

Effectiveness: 79% (Typical) - 95% (Perfect)

Can be inserted hours prior, providing STI protection and autonomy.

C. Hormonal Methods

Chemical regulation of ovulation. These methods manipulate the biological environment to prevent egg release.

Oral Contraceptives

Requires daily administration at the exact same time. Highly effective when discipline is maintained (99% perfect use).

Transdermal Patch

Applied to the skin, releasing hormones continuously. Replaced weekly for three weeks, followed by a patch-free week.

Vaginal Ring

Flexible ring inserted for three weeks. Self-administered and highly effective.

D. Long-Acting Reversible (LARC)

Set-and-forget protocols. These provide the highest tier of defense with the lowest requirement for user discipline.

IUD (Intrauterine Device)

Effective for 3 to 10 years depending on the type (Hormonal or Copper). Effectiveness: >99%.

The Implant

A small rod inserted under the arm skin. Effective for up to 3 years. Effectiveness: >99%.

G. Myths vs Facts

MYTH

You can't get pregnant the first time.

FACT

Pregnancy can occur any time ovulation happens and sperm is present, regardless of experience.

MYTH

Withdrawal (pulling out) is highly effective.

FACT

Pre-ejaculate can carry sperm. Typical use effectiveness is only around 78%.

H. Visual Intelligences

Emergency Protocols

ACTION REQUIRED: Post-Exposure Defense

If primary defenses fail, emergency contraception (Plan B, Copper IUD) can be deployed.

  • Morning-After Pill: Most effective within 72 hours. Delays ovulation.
  • Copper IUD: Can be inserted up to 5 days after unprotected sex. 99% effective as emergency contraception.
